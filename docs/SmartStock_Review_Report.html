2. Introduction: What is the Problem?

Modern retail businesses struggle with inventory misalignment caused by volatile customer shopping habits and supplier delivery unpredictability:

  • Stockouts: Running out of merchandise leads to immediate lost sales, broken customer trust, and market share loss to competitors.
  • Overstocking: Holding excessive merchandise ties up working capital, fills expensive warehouse racks, and increases inventory spoilage or obsolescence.
  • The Bullwhip Effect: Minor changes in customer checkout registers cause violent, magnified demand swings upstream at regional distributors and factory lines.
Core Limitation of Existing Systems: Most small-to-medium retail operations rely on static reorder thresholds (e.g., ordering 20 units whenever stock drops below 10) or manual manager intuition, failing to handle weekend traffic surges or variable supplier delays.
Section 3

3. Why is it a Problem? (Mathematical Formulation)

Given $S = 10$ retail stores and $I = 50$ items, we manage $N = 500$ daily time-series $\{y_{s,i,t}\}$ across $T = 1,826$ days. Daily sales $D_t \sim (\mu_d, \sigma_d)$ and replenishment lead time $L \sim (\mu_L, \sigma_L)$ are non-deterministic stochastic variables.

The total operating penalty that our optimization engine minimizes is:

$$\text{Total Cost} = \sum_{t=1}^T \Big( C_{\text{lost}} \cdot \max(0, D_t - I_t) + C_{\text{hold}} \cdot \max(0, I_t - D_t) \Big)$$

Where $I_t$ is stock on hand at day $t$, $C_{\text{lost}}$ is stockout cost, and $C_{\text{hold}}$ is inventory holding cost.

Section 8

8. Preprocessing & Data Preparation Pipeline

To eliminate training data contamination without lookahead leakage, all anomaly group statistics are fit strictly on training records ($t \le \text{2017-10-01}$):

  1. Weekday-Group Z-Score: $$Z = \frac{y_t - \mu_{s,i,w}}{\sigma_{s,i,w}} \quad (\text{Flagged if } |Z| > 3.0)$$
  2. Tukey Interquartile Range (IQR): $$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1, \quad \text{Fences} = [Q_1 - 1.5\cdot\text{IQR}, \; Q_3 + 1.5\cdot\text{IQR}]$$
  3. Cleaned Series Median Imputation: If flagged as an anomaly, the noisy value is replaced with the robust group median ($M_{s,i,w}$), preserving trend without spike bias.
Section 9

9. AI Problem Solving: Algorithms & Mathematics

A. Damped Holt-Winters Exponential Smoothing (DS-1)

Forecasts demand using additive level ($\ell_t$), damped trend ($b_t$), and multiplicative weekly seasonality ($s_t$):

$$\hat{y}_{t+h} = \left(\ell_t + \sum_{j=1}^h \phi^j b_t\right) s_{t+h-m} \quad (\text{where } m=7, \; \phi \approx 0.98)$$

B. Extended King's Safety Stock & Dynamic Reorder Point (AI-1)

Calculates exact buffer inventory accounting for simultaneous demand and delivery variance:

$$\mathbf{SS} = Z_\alpha \sqrt{\mu_L \cdot \sigma_d^2 + \mu_d^2 \cdot \sigma_L^2}$$ $$\mathbf{ROP} = (\mu_d \times \mu_L) + SS$$

Where service factors $Z_\alpha$ are tied to SKU classification tiers: Class A = 98% ($Z=2.054$), Class B = 95% ($Z=1.645$), Class C = 90% ($Z=1.282$).
